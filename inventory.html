SUPPLY CHAIN MANAGEMENT / LEARNING CASE

Inventory & Replenishment Planning

Hypothetical educational case with simulated inputs. Prepared with AI assistance for Hao Zeng’s review and learning. This is not employment experience, client work, or evidence of implemented business results. All conclusions are model outputs, not observed outcomes.

Business question

When should a hypothetical distributor reorder a single imported spare part, and how does a longer replenishment lead time change the stock requirement?

Simulated model inputs

InputAssumed value
Mean daily demand100 units
Daily demand standard deviation20 units
Baseline replenishment lead time9 days, fixed
Target cycle service level95%
Normal safety factor z1.645
Longer-lead-time scenario16 days, fixed

These parameters are assumed, not estimates from real sales records. Daily demand is independent and approximately normal. There are no seasonal shifts, supply quantity shortages, or random lead times. Inventory is reviewed continuously.

Calculations

Safety stock = z × daily demand standard deviation × √lead time, rounded up to a whole unit.

Reorder point = mean daily demand × lead time + safety stock.

Baseline safety stock = ceil(1.645 × 20 × √9) = ceil(98.7) = 99 units.

Baseline reorder point = 100 × 9 + 99 = 999 units.

Interpretation

Reorder at an inventory position of 999 units

Inventory position means on-hand stock + stock on order − backorders. For example, 700 on hand + 350 on order − 60 backordered = 990 units, which is below the reorder point and triggers replenishment.

Moving from 9 to 16 days raises the reorder point by 733 units: 700 from additional expected demand and 33 from additional safety stock. This is not a claim that average on-hand inventory rises by 733 units.

A 95% cycle service target means approximately a 95% chance of no stockout during a replenishment cycle under these assumptions. It is not the same as filling 95% of units demanded.

Recommendation and limitations

Use 999 units as the baseline trigger in this teaching scenario. Review demand variability and lead times before applying the rule. If lead time becomes variable or demand is correlated, the formula needs adjustment. Order quantity, ordering cost, holding cost, and capacity limits are outside this model. No actual reduction in stockouts has been measured.

Skills demonstrated by the case

Safety-stock calculation, reorder-point planning, service-level interpretation, and sensitivity analysis.
