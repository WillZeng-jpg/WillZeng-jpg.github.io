SUPPLY CHAIN MANAGEMENT / LEARNING CASE

Global Supply Chain Risk Assessment

Hypothetical educational case with simulated inputs. Prepared with AI assistance for Hao Zeng’s review and learning. This is not employment experience, client work, or evidence of implemented business results. All conclusions are model outputs, not observed outcomes.

Business question

How should a fictional importer prioritize disruptions in a supply chain for electronic accessories?

Illustrative flow: component vendors → overseas assembly → export port → ocean shipment → destination port → US warehouse → customers. The model assumes one assembly supplier accounts for 80% of supply and the primary ocean route passes through one port.

Scoring method

Likelihood and impact are subjective ratings from 1 (low) to 5 (high). Priority score = likelihood × impact. Scores 15–25 are high, 8–14 medium, and 1–7 low. These are scenario-specific categories, not industry standards or calibrated probabilities. Impact reflects operational interruption, not a measured financial loss.

Stress test: supplier outage

Assume demand is 100 units/day, the primary supplier provides 80 units/day, and an unaffected supplier provides 20 units/day. A 14-day primary outage creates a gross supply gap of 80 × 14 = 1,120 units.

With 600 units of usable buffer stock already on hand, the uncovered gap is max(0, 1,120 − 600) = 520 units. This assumes the unaffected flow continues and all buffer stock can serve the same product.

A proposed third supplier delivering an additional 40 units/day on outage days 8–14 would provide 40 × 7 = 280 units. The remaining modeled gap would be 520 − 280 = 240 units. This response reduces the uncovered gap by 53.8%, but does not eliminate it.

Recommendation and limitations

Prioritize the two high-score risks, while testing whether backup capacity can arrive before buffer inventory runs out. The scores organize discussion; ordinal numbers do not measure expected loss. Costs, route dependencies, and the chance of simultaneous disruptions require further analysis. The action plan has not been implemented.

Skills demonstrated by the case

Supply chain mapping, risk prioritization, disruption arithmetic, and mitigation planning.
